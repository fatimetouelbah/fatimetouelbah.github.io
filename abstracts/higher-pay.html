Abstract

Higher Pay or Smaller Classes? Retaining Teachers in Hard-to-Staff Schools

Fatimetou El Bah, 2026

This paper studies the effects of higher pay and better working conditions on teacher retention in disadvantaged schools. I exploit two French reforms: a financial bonus introduced in 2015 and subsequently increased for the most disadvantaged schools, and a class size reduction progressively rolled out from 2017, capping first- and second-grade classes at approximately half their pre-reform size. Because some teachers were exposed to the bonus alone while others were also exposed to class size reduction, comparing retention across the two groups isolates the effect of the bonus and shows how retention evolves when class size reduction is added to it. Using an event study design, I find that the bonus exhibits a threshold: a bonus of up to 6% of annual salary has no detectable effect on retention, while a bonus reaching approximately 10% increases retention by 2 to 3 percentage points. Primary grade teachers, who were exposed to both treatments, show the largest gains, up to 4 percentage points. These findings suggest that both higher pay and better working conditions can help stabilize the teaching workforce in disadvantaged schools, with the largest gains where the two operate together.
